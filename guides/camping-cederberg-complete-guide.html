Camping in the Cederberg

When to go, where to camp, what to hike — the complete guide to South Africa's wildest mountain wilderness

Home › Guides › Cederberg Camping Guide

The Cederberg Wilderness Area covers roughly 71,000 hectares of sandstone peaks, hidden valleys, and ancient riverbeds 200km north of Cape Town. It’s the kind of place that changes your idea of what the Western Cape actually is.

When to Go

Autumn (March–May) is the consensus favourite: cooler temperatures, no crowds, crystal-clear air after the first autumn rains, and the orange-red sandstone glowing in afternoon light. Spring (September–October) brings wildflowers and the best conditions for long hikes. Summer (December–January) can be extremely hot — avoid for multi-day hikes. Winter (June–August) means cold nights (near-zero), possible snow on the peaks, and empty camps.

Where to Stay

  • Kleinfontein — basic sites, no electricity, rock art walks. The real Cederberg.
  • Kardoesie, Piekenierskloof — dramatic gorge setting on the N7. Good first night.
  • Algeria Campsite (Cape Nature) — the main official campsite near Citrusdal. Best for families and first-timers.
  • Sanddrif Holiday Resort — privately run on the Rondegat River. More facilities, popular in December.

Key Hikes

  • Maltese Cross — 4.5km to a 20-metre sandstone cross. Iconic.
  • Wolfberg Arch — one of the most dramatic natural structures in the Western Cape. Hard route (~7km return).
  • Wolfberg Cracks — narrow gorge passages requiring some squeezing. Unique and fun.
  • Sneeuberg Peak (2,028m) — the highest point in the Western Cape. Full-day undertaking.
  • Rock Art Trail — San Bushman paintings thousands of years old. Several accessible near Kleinfontein.

What to Bring

  • Warm sleeping bag — nights drop sharply at altitude even in summer
  • Solid hiking boots — sandstone is rough and slippery when wet
  • Sufficient water — many streams dry up in summer
  • Paper map or downloaded offline maps — cell coverage is unreliable
  • Firewood from Citrusdal — collecting wood in the wilderness area is prohibited

Getting There

Kleinfontein is approximately 230km from Cape Town via the N7 (Citrusdal) then a gravel road. Allow 3–3.5 hours. Fill up with fuel in Citrusdal — there are no fuel stations once you turn off the N7.

Permits and Booking

The Cederberg Wilderness Area is managed by Cape Nature. Overnight hikers need a permit, bookable via CapeNature.co.za. Private farms book directly. Book early for December, January, and Easter — popular camps fill months in advance.
