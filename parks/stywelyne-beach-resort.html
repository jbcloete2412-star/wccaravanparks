About Stywelyne Beach Resort

Municipal resort at the mouth of the Berg River on the West Coast, near the world-famous bokkoms (dried fish) of Velddrif. 74 electrified caravan and camping stands, self-catering chalets, two swimming pools and a children's playground. Excellent for angling, crayfish diving and birdwatching along the estuary.

Facilities & What to Expect

Stywelyne Beach Resort in Velddrif sits at the mouth of the Berg River where it meets the West Coast — 74 electric sites with ablution facilities, braai areas, and direct river and lagoon access. The resort is immersed in the unique culture of the Velddrif fishing community, famous across South Africa for bokkoms — wind-dried harders (mullet) that are a local delicacy with a powerfully pungent character. The Berg River estuary is one of the bird-richest wetlands in the Western Cape, adding a serious nature dimension to the camping experience.

When to Visit

November and December is harder season — the annual run of harders up the Berg River estuary is a unique natural spectacle and the bokkoms industry is at peak activity. July through September brings exceptional birdwatching as lesser flamingos, African spoonbills, and thousands of waders congregate in the estuary. Summer (December to February) is beach and river season. Winter is the quietest period but the birdlife makes it far from dull.

What to Do Nearby

The Berg River is the main stage — boat trips and self-paddled canoes navigate the channels through dense reedbed with astonishing birdlife. Flamingo counts in the estuary regularly exceed 5,000 birds during peak season. The Velddrif bokkoms industry is a local institution — tour the drying racks and buy directly from producers. Langebaan (25km south) and West Coast National Park (30km south) are easy day trips. Paternoster (40km north) is a jewel of a whitewashed fishing village.

Getting There

Take the R27 (West Coast Road) north from Cape Town (150km) through Langebaan and on to Velddrif. The town is just north of Laaiplek at the Berg River mouth. Total drive time from Cape Town is approximately 1.5 to 2 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is bokkoms?

Bokkoms is a traditional wind-dried salted harder (mullet) — a food preparation with roots in the West Coast fishing communities of the 17th and 18th centuries. The dried fish has an extremely strong smell and flavour that is an acquired taste but beloved in the Velddrif community. Velddrif is the only significant bokkoms-producing town left in South Africa.

Is the Berg River estuary good for kayaking?

The Berg River estuary around Velddrif is excellent for kayaking — flat water, sheltered channels through reedbed, and extraordinary birdlife within arm's reach. It is one of the best kayaking environments in the Western Cape for beginners because there is no open ocean exposure. Bring your own kayak or check with the resort and local operators for hire options.

What birds can I see at the Berg River mouth?

The Berg River estuary is one of the Western Cape's most important wetlands — lesser flamingo, African spoonbill, African sacred ibis, great white pelican, pied avocet, black-necked grebe, and numerous wader species are regularly recorded. July through September typically produces the highest bird counts when northern hemisphere migrant waders arrive.
