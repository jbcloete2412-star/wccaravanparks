About Tietiesbaai Rest Camp

60 raw seafront campsites on the wild Atlantic inside the Cape Columbine Nature Reserve — the furthest westerly point in the Western Cape. No electricity (solar only for ablutions). The last manually operated lighthouse in South Africa stands on Castle Rock above. Legendary for crayfish diving, perlemoen, rock fishing and Namaqualand spring flowers. In-season bookings via Saldanha Bay Municipality; off-season first come, first served. Pure, untouched West Coast.

Facilities & What to Expect

Tietiesbaai Rest Camp at Cape Columbine is as elemental as South African camping gets — 60 sites on a rocky coastal headland with a lighthouse, no electricity, basic ablutions, and the full force of the West Coast wind and sea as your companions. This is CapeNature-managed wilderness camping in the purest sense. Bring a gas stove, a windproof tent, torches for the (extremely dark) nights, and enough supplies for your entire stay. What you receive in return is one of the most dramatically beautiful campsites in the Western Cape, direct rock-pool access, and total detachment from ordinary life.

When to Visit

August through November is prime time — spring wildflowers transform the Cape Columbine Nature Reserve into a carpet of colour, sea conditions are calmer than winter, and the camp is operating but not yet at its Christmas peak capacity. December through early January the camp fills dramatically — many campers queue from 3am for a site that operates on a first-come, first-served basis during peak season. June and July brings winter storms — challenging but atmospheric if you are properly equipped.

What to Do Nearby

Rock fishing and spearfishing from the headland is the primary outdoor activity. The rock pools at the base of the lighthouse headland are exceptional and among the best in the Western Cape for tidal pool life. Paternoster village (12km north) is a National Monument fishing village with excellent seafood restaurants serving fresh crayfish and paternoster fish. West Coast National Park (50km south) has the Langebaan Lagoon and spring wildflowers. Strandveld hiking within the reserve is basic but rewarding.

Getting There

From Cape Town take the R27 north to Velddrif, then continue north to Paternoster via the R45 and gravel roads — approximately 160km. From Paternoster, follow the gravel road 12km south to Cape Columbine and the rest camp. The final section requires a high-clearance vehicle, particularly after rain. Total drive time from Cape Town is approximately 2.5 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Tietiesbaai Rest Camp have electricity?

No — Tietiesbaai operates with no grid electricity. This is off-grid camping: bring gas for cooking, battery-powered lights, power banks for devices, and a solar panel if you are camping for more than a few days. The pitch-black night sky with zero light pollution is extraordinary compensation.

How do I secure a campsite at Tietiesbaai over Christmas?

Peak season (late December to early January) operates on a first-come, first-served basis. Campers begin arriving the evening before or in the early hours to queue for a site. Arriving at 4am is not unusual. For all other dates, pre-booking is possible through CapeNature. Confirm the current booking system as CapeNature periodically adjusts peak-season procedures.

What is the Cape Columbine lighthouse?

The Cape Columbine lighthouse, built in 1936, is the last manually operated lighthouse in South Africa. It stands on the headland adjacent to the rest camp and is visible for miles at sea. The lighthouse remains operational and the headland walk around it is spectacular, with extraordinary views over the Atlantic.
