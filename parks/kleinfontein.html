About Kleinfontein

A scenic mountain campsite tucked into the northern Cederberg, renowned for its star-gazing (some of the darkest skies in South Africa), ancient rock art, dramatic rock formations and excellent hiking. The Cederberg is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve rich in fynbos, Bushman paintings and endangered Clanwilliam cedar trees.

Facilities & What to Expect

Kleinfontein is a private farm campsite deep in the Cederberg Conservancy — as far from resort camping as it is possible to get. The main site has basic but functional ablutions and some electric sites; remote satellite camps on the farm are truly off-grid with cold water only and pit-style facilities. Bring gas for cooking, your own water purification if camping at remote sites, and a tent that handles wind and cold. What you receive in return is extraordinary: some of the darkest skies in South Africa, ancient San rock art within walking distance, and complete silence.

When to Visit

March through May and August through October are ideal. Winter (June to August) nights in the Cederberg can be bitterly cold (sub-zero at altitude) but the star-gazing conditions are exceptional — absolutely zero light pollution and the Milky Way directly overhead. Summer (December to January) is very hot and fire risk is high. Spring brings spectacular fynbos flowering with proteas covering the mountain slopes.

What to Do Nearby

Rock art viewing is the primary reason most visitors make the effort to reach Kleinfontein — Stadsaal Caves (within the Cederberg Wilderness Area, accessible via permit from CapeNature) has some of the finest San paintings in the Western Cape. The Maltese Cross (an iconic rock formation, about a 3-hour return hike from Dwarsrivier Farm, 20km away) is a Cederberg institution. The Cederberg Wilderness Area requires an overnight hiking permit from CapeNature. Stargazing on the farm is extraordinary — bring binoculars for the night sky.

Getting There

From Cape Town take the N7 to Clanwilliam (240km), then follow the R303 into the Cederberg toward Algeria. Kleinfontein is accessible via farm roads — request detailed directions from the farm on booking, as GPS can be unreliable in the mountains. A high-clearance vehicle is recommended; 4WD is advisable after rain.

Frequently Asked Questions

How dark are the skies at Kleinfontein for stargazing?

The Cederberg is recognised as one of the best stargazing locations in South Africa, with Bortle Class 2-3 darkness (exceptionally dark). On a clear moonless night, the naked-eye Milky Way is strikingly visible and thousands of stars are visible without any optical aid. This is a serious location for astrophotography.

What is the San rock art near Kleinfontein like?

The Cederberg rock art tradition spans thousands of years. The paintings depict San hunting scenes, spiritual and trance visions, and wildlife including eland, rhino, and human figures. Many sites are accessible on foot from Kleinfontein farm with farm guide assistance. Some significant sites require CapeNature permits.

Is Kleinfontein suitable for first-time campers?

Kleinfontein is better suited to experienced campers comfortable with basic facilities and self-sufficiency. If you have never camped without electricity or running hot water, this is a challenging introduction. For families or novices seeking Cederberg access with more amenities, Citrusdal or graded CapeNature campsites are better starting points.
